We are on the 5th day of Sukkot with our guest of honor today, Aharon Aaron. Shalom, shalom. The Torah. &gt;&gt; You all know the Midrash; we have already cited it regarding the choice God made with these four species that we know well. Why these and not a chili pepper, as the Gemara asks? Everything represents something we have already discussed in the Midrash: the scent, the taste. Today, another excellent dimension in honor of Aharon Hakohen. So, first, we will take the Lulav. The Lulav, as you know, can have dozens of lulavim on a palm tree, but they all come from the same single trunk, just like the Jewish people. We can have many different opinions, many styles, many ideas, but ultimately, we are all from the same branch of Abraham Avinu. Next, we continue with the only fruit in the world—one of the reasons it is called Hadar, as the Gemara says, is that it dwells ("dar") in the tree all year long. This means it endures rain —even today with the rain—storms, and heat; it is always capable of holding onto the tree. Every Jew, each of us, goes through storms during the year. For some, it is in health, for another, in their married life. For one , it is in their livelihood. Sometimes there are moments where it is beautiful , where there is sun; sometimes there is wind. But you must hold onto the tree and not let go of the trunk all year round. We will then move on to the Hadas. As we know from the Midrash, it is compared to the eyes; it has a good scent. Thank God, we too sometimes meet people; they have a good eye, they have a good scent, even if they aren't very solid—I mean, it doesn't hold up for very long. But it is a friendship that can sometimes last a few months, or sometimes it is enough that you met someone who has a good eye like the Hadas and left you with a good scent, and it becomes a friendship and we love each other. And the last one, Rabotai, the Aravah. You see, it is already dry. I think I need to change it; it is already struggling a bit. It is tired, but it is part of the Jewish people. What does the Aravah represent in the Midrash? It is the mouth. The rava represents those people you sometimes meet while waiting for the doctor or on the bus. There are people you only know for 10 minutes, but in those 10 minutes , ah, you know so-and-so, and we’ve become best friends. Even if 10 minutes later we don’t know each other anymore. That friendship just faded away. It just fell apart. But that’s part of the Jewish people. It’s unique. It’s only with us that I can see someone for 10 minutes. I can go through their whole life story with them and we love each other as if we’ve known each other for years, even if in a minute the 3 minutes are over.
